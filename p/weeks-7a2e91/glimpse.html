2. Your website hides your best points

  • On the Contact page your email address, info@weekswells.com, is linked to your phone number. Tapping the email starts a phone call, not an email.
  • The Maine Ground Water Association badge at the foot of every page opens a parked web address, not the Association.
  • On the Services page, five of the six logos under "Check Out These Links" open missing pages. Only the Maine.gov logo works.
  • On the home page your phone number is plain text, not a call link. There is no form anywhere to ask for an estimate.
  • Your service area is shown only as a picture of a map. Kennebec County is the only place named in words. No towns are listed.
  • Your hours appear only on the Contact page.
  • The site says "60+ years" and "more than 60 years". You started in 1959, so it is now 67.
  • Typing slips: "COMMERICIAL" and "the the cost" on the home page, "shallow ell hand pumps" on Services, "Each foot of hole store" on About Your Well.
  • The Gallery has 12 good photos with no captions. Their hidden descriptions are file names such as "01 new slide.jpg".
  • The home page has no main heading, and the site has no site map and no business details in the form search engines read.

We have already built an optimized front page that fixes these, using only your own words, photos and logo. Your other pages stay as they are. The link is in our email.

3. Credentials customers never see

  • The National Ground Water Association directory lists Timothy Williams at your firm with the CVCLD mark (Certified Vertical Closed Loop Driller). It is not on your site.
  • The Better Business Bureau gives you an A+ rating (as it appeared in search results on 14 November 2026). It is not on your site.
  • Your site names your State of Maine licences but shows no licence numbers and no link to the state list of licensed well drillers. Customers and lenders look for both.
  • Your site does not name a single person. A family firm since 1959 is a strong point, and customers like to know who will turn up.

4. Google and reviews

  • Three directory sites show you at 3.4 stars from 14 reviews. One of them says the figure is based on Google reviews. A fourth site shows 3.5 stars from 13 reviews.
  • With so few reviews, each new one counts for a lot. A simple card or text asking each finished customer for a Google review is the fix.
  • Your own site has four kind customer notes, but no dates on them and no link to your Google reviews.
  • We found no Facebook page for the business, and your site links to none.

We did not open Google itself. Please check your Google Business Profile for the true figures today.
